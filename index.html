Hi, I'm Yijin Zeng (曾一津).

I'm a machine learning scientist at Expedia Group, working on Expedia's B2B Revenue Optimisation & Data Science Team. Prior to this, I completed my PhD in Statistics and Machine Learning at Imperial College London, jointly with the University of Oxford through the StatML CDT program.

My PhD research focused on developing rigorous statistical methodologies for hypothesis testing, correlation analysis, and change-point detection in the presence of missing data. You can find my publications in statistics and machine learning journals.

My goal is to be an excellent machine learining scientist and I have been having so much fun during this process. At Expedia, I take ownership of end-to-end ML pipelines and drive innovation in our technical solutions. Outside of work, I love diving deep into different topics such as LLMs, generative AI, computer science, and mathematics. Some of the topics I have learned so far can be found in my notes, along with my handwritten PDFs. I also enjoy getting my hands dirty by building various fun machine learning projects. In addition, I am very active for various engagements such as tech talks, teaching, and paper reading sessions.

Papers

Exact Bounds of Spearman's Footrule in the Presence of Missing Data with Applications to Independence Testing
Yijin Zeng, Niall M. Adams, Dean A. Bodenham
Electron. J. Statist. 19(2): 6103-6140 (2025) · 2025
This work derives mathematical tight bounds for Spearman's footrule (a robust alternative to Spearman's rank correlation) in the presence of missing data, proposed efficient algorithms for computing the bounds, and applies the bounds independence testing with missing data.

Yijin Zeng, Niall M. Adams, Dean A. Bodenham
Transactions on Machine Learning Research (11/2025) · 2024
This work extends the popular maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) two-sample testing methods to the cases where data might be missing or partially observed.

"温故而知新，可以为师矣" — 孔子
Review the old to understand the new.

I maintain these notes for several reasons. First, my memory isn't perfect; something I understood completely a few months ago can easily slip away. Whenever I need to recall specific information, reviewing my own notes is always faster than digging back through the original source. Additionally, it is deeply satisfying to watch my personal library of knowledge expand. Learning is enjoyable on its own, but seeing tangible proof of my growth is a powerful motivation to keep going. Last but not least, I believe these notes might eventually help others. Sharing knowledge and connecting with people is a massive source of happiness for me, and I truly hope this collection can be a valuable resource for you, too.
