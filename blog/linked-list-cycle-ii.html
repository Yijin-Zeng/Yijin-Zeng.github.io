142. Linked List Cycle II

Leetcode

A concise handwritten version of this note is available as a PDF.

Floyd's cycle detection is one of those algorithms where the "reset to head" trick at the end feels like magic until you work through the math. The problem: given a linked list with a cycle, find exactly where the cycle begins using only O(1) extra space.

Problem Setup

Consider a linked list that has a "tail" of length m (the non-cyclic portion before the cycle entry) and a cycle of length n. We use two pointers:

  • Slow pointer: moves 1 step at a time
  • Fast pointer: moves 2 steps at a time

Both start at the head (position 1). After t steps, the slow pointer is at position 1 + t, and the fast pointer is at position 1 + 2t.

Positions Within the Cycle

Once a pointer enters the cycle, its position wraps around. Any position in the cycle can be described as m - 1 + n*k + i, where i is the offset within the cycle (with the entry node at offset 0) and k = 0, 1, 2, ... counts full laps around the cycle.

Finding Where They First Meet

When the slow and fast pointers first meet inside the cycle, both are at the same physical node. At time t:

Slow pointer position: 1 + t
Fast pointer position: 1 + 2t

For them to be at the same location in the cycle:
  m - 1 + n*k1 + i = 1 + t      ... (slow)
  m - 1 + n*k2 + i = 1 + 2t     ... (fast)

Subtracting the first equation from the second:

n*(k2 - k1) = t

This means t is a multiple of the cycle length n.

Constraint on k2 - k1

Since the fast pointer must have entered the cycle before meeting (i.e., t >= m - 1), we need:

n*(k2 - k1) >= m - 1
=>  k2 - k1 >= (m - 1) / n

Deriving the Meeting Offset

From the slow pointer equation:

m - 1 + n*k1 + i = 1 + n*(k2 - k1)
=>  i = 2 + n*(k2 - k1) - n*k1 - m

To find the earliest meeting point, let k2 - k1 = ceil((m-1)/n) = C and let k1 = 0 (slow pointer has not completed a full lap yet). Then:

i = 2 + n*C - m

where C = ceil((m - 1) / n)

So the key result: the meeting offset within the cycle is i = 2 + n * ceil((m-1)/n) - m.

Example

If m = 10 and n = 7:

C = ceil((10 - 1) / 7) = ceil(9/7) = 2
i = 2 + 7*2 - 10 = 6

They meet at offset 6 inside the cycle (i.e., 6 nodes past the entry point).

Finding the Cycle Entry

After the two pointers meet, how do we find the cycle entry? The meeting point is at offset i inside the cycle. To get back to the entry (offset 0), we need to advance by n - i steps within the cycle:

n - i = n - (2 + n*C - m) = n - 2 - n*C + m = m - 2 + n*(1 - C)

When C = ceil((m-1)/n), this simplifies to:
  steps to entry from meeting point = m - 1 + n * (1 - C)
                                     ... but more usefully:
  = (m - 1) mod n  (effectively)

The important insight: after the pointers meet, reset one pointer to the head of the list. Then advance both pointers one step at a time. They will meet exactly at the cycle entry node after m - 1 steps.

Why the "Reset to Head" Trick Works

After the meeting, consider:

  • One pointer starts at the head and takes m - 1 steps to reach the cycle entry.
  • The other pointer is at offset i in the cycle. After m - 1 more steps, it will be at offset i + (m - 1) mod n in the cycle.

Since i = 2 + n*C - m, we get:

i + (m - 1) = 2 + n*C - m + m - 1 = 1 + n*C

(1 + n*C) mod n = 1 mod n ...

Wait -- let us be more careful with the indexing. The entry node is at position m from the head (1-indexed). So from the head, it takes exactly m - 1 steps to reach the entry. The pointer in the cycle at offset i needs n - i steps to return to offset 0 (the entry). The formula shows that n - i = n - 2 - n*C + m. When the tail length equals or exceeds the cycle, this works out to m - 1 steps (modulo full cycles). Both pointers therefore arrive at the entry simultaneously.

In summary: after the meeting, one pointer goes back to the start of the list. Both pointers advance one step at a time. The number of steps until they meet again equals exactly the distance to the cycle entry -- that is, 1 + n * ceil((m-1)/n) steps from the meeting point perspective, which coincides with the m - 1 steps from the head.

Algorithm

  1. Initialize slow and fast pointers at the head.
  2. Move slow by 1, fast by 2, until they meet (confirms a cycle exists).
  3. Reset one pointer to the head.
  4. Move both pointers by 1 step at a time until they meet again.
  5. The meeting node is the cycle entry.

Implementation

class Solution:
    def detectCycle(self, head):
        slow = fast = head

        # Phase 1: Detect cycle
        while fast and fast.next:
            slow = slow.next
            fast = fast.next.next
            if slow == fast:
                break
        else:
            return None  # No cycle

        # Phase 2: Find entry
        slow = head
        while slow != fast:
            slow = slow.next
            fast = fast.next

        return slow

Complexity

  • Time: O(n) -- both phases are linear in the number of nodes.
  • Space: O(1) -- only two pointers used.
