PLU Decomposition and Implementation

How to Solve a Linear System?

We want to solve $Ax = b$. As an example, take

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 & -2 \\ 4 & 9 & -3 \\ -2 & -3 & 7 \end{bmatrix} \qquad b = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 8 \\ 10 \end{bmatrix}$$

The idea is to do Gaussian elimination first, so the equation becomes

$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix} x = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix}$$

Then we can do backward substitution to solve $x_1, x_2, x_3$ easily:

$$\begin{aligned} 4x_3 &= 8 &&\Rightarrow x_3 = 2 \\ x_2 + x_3 &= 4 &&\Rightarrow x_2 = 2 \\ 2x_1 + 4x_2 - 2x_3 &= 2 &&\Rightarrow x_1 = -1 \end{aligned}$$

Gaussian Elimination

Going back to the same example, write $A$ and $b$ together as an augmented matrix and eliminate row by row.

$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 4 & -2 & 2 \\ 4 & 9 & -3 & 8 \\ -2 & -3 & 7 & 10 \end{array}\right]$$

Second row − 2×first row:

$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 4 & -2 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 4 \\ -2 & -3 & 7 & 10 \end{array}\right]$$

Third row + first row:

$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 4 & -2 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 & 5 & 12 \end{array}\right]$$

Third row − second row:

$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 4 & -2 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 & 8 \end{array}\right]$$

That's the upper triangular system we used above.

Each Elimination Step Has a Simple Matrix Representation

Each of the row operations above can be written as left-multiplication by a matrix. For instance, "second row − 2×first row" is

$$E_{21} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

LU Decomposition

To generalize, we have

$$E_{n,n-1} \cdots E_{m1} \cdots E_{21} A = U$$

where the product of elimination matrices on the left are the elimination steps, and $U$ is the resulting upper triangular matrix. Inverting both sides,

$$A = E_{21}^{-1} E_{m1}^{-1} \cdots E_{n,n-1}^{-1} U$$

Result 1: if $E_{ij}$ is the identity matrix with $L_{ij}$ at entry $(i,j)$, then $E_{ij}^{-1}$ is the identity matrix with $-L_{ij}$ at entry $(i,j)$.

Result 2: the product $E_{21}^{-1} E_{31}^{-1} \cdots E_{n,n-1}^{-1}$ is a single lower triangular matrix with all the $-L_{ij}$ entries stacked in their respective positions, e.g.

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -L_{21} & 1 & 0 \\ -L_{31} & -L_{32} & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

We can prove this by induction: multiplying $E_{ij}^{-1}$ (which has $-L_{ij}$ at entry $(i,j)$ and zeros elsewhere off the diagonal) into the product so far only fills in entry $(i,j)$ with $-L_{ij}$, since the entry at $(i,j)$ in the partial product is still zero at that point and any other entries below the diagonal are left untouched.

The effect of this matrix: a Gaussian elimination step takes the $i$-th row, times $L_{ij}$, and adds it to the $j$-th row, where $j > i$. So this operation only affects entry $(i,j)$.

So we have $A = LU$. This is the known LU decomposition.

LU Decomposition Isn't Always Possible

For example, if

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 3 & 5 \\ 4 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

then the first row cannot eliminate the second or third row, because the $(1,1)$ entry is $0$.

Define the row exchange matrix $P_{ij}$ as the identity matrix with rows $i$ and $j$ swapped. The effect of this matrix: $P_{ij} A$ swaps rows $A_i$ and $A_j$ of $A$.

An important property of $P_{ij}$: $P_{ij} = P_{ij}^{-1}$. This is obvious because $P_{ij}^2 = I$ — swap rows $i$ and $j$, then swap them again, and we're back where we started.

PLU Decomposition

So in many cases we have a chain that mixes elimination steps and row exchanges, something like $(E \cdots P E \cdots P E \cdots E) A = U$, and we want to collect all the $P$'s together. Since $PE = E'P$ where $E' = PEP$, each time we push a permutation matrix $P$ through an elimination matrix $E$, $E$ turns into a new elimination matrix $E'$ acting on the permuted row indices.

Concretely, for a chain like $E_{32} P_{23} E_{31} E_{21}$:

$$\begin{aligned} E_{32} P_{23} E_{31} E_{21} &= E_{32} E'_{31} P_{23} E_{21} \\ &= E_{32} E'_{31} E'_{21} P_{23} \end{aligned}$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} E'_{31} &= P_{23} E_{31} P_{23} \\ &= P_{23} (I + L_{31} e_3 e_1^\top) P_{23} \\ &= I + L_{31} P_{23} e_3 e_1^\top P_{23} \\ &= I + L_{31} e_2 e_1^\top \qquad (\text{goes to row 2}) \end{aligned}$$

and similarly

$$E'_{21} = I + L_{21} e_3 e_1^\top \qquad (\text{goes to row 3})$$

Putting it back together, $E_{32} P_{23} E_{31} E_{21} A = U$ becomes $E_{32} E'_{31} E'_{21} (P_{23} A) = U$, so

$$P_{23} A = (E_{32} E'_{31} E'_{21})^{-1} U = LU$$

This is the PLU decomposition: $PA = LU$.
